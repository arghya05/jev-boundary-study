\section{Measurement and Statistical Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\subsection{Execution and fairness controls}
All clients, scorers and analysis ran on a single RunPod pod (NVIDIA A40, 48\,GB, \$0.59/h) that also served JEV-9B with vLLM~0.31.0~\citep{vllm}.
Within each item the provider order was randomised with a seeded generator.
The SDKs' own retries were disabled; the runner retries at most once, and only for a transport failure or an HTTP~408/409/429/5xx, never because of an answer.
Refusals, truncations, invalid outputs and errors stay in every denominator as incorrect.
Responses are cached under a key that hashes the task content, model, version, effort, output schema and protocol version, so no item is ever billed twice and no answer is ever chosen among repeats.
Every paid request reserves its configured maximum cost in an append-only ledger before dispatch and settles its billed cost afterwards.
Before any held-out call, development usage was used to forecast each panel's expected and conservative (95th-percentile) cost; a run stops for review if spending would exceed 1.5 times the conservative forecast, or if any system's failure rate on a panel exceeds 10\%.

\subsection{Cost accounting}
Frontier cost is billed usage times dated list prices: \$10 per million input tokens and \$50 per million output tokens for both providers, where output includes all reasoning or thinking tokens (we verified that neither provider double-reports reasoning).
A batch view at half these prices is reported as a derived figure; we did not run batch jobs, since at measured per-item costs the discount changes no conclusion.
JEV-9B's cost is GPU time: the pod's hourly price multiplied by measured wall-clock.
For System~1 we report per-call cost from sequential latency, which overstates cost under batching; for System~2, whose requests were batched up to 112 at a time, we report pod-hours over the panel's run window.
As context only, we also price System~1 inputs at official Jev's \$0.042 per million input tokens; no official-Jev bill exists in this study.

\subsection{Statistics}
\label{sec:stats}
Let $\Delta=\mathrm{acc}(\text{JEV-9B})-\mathrm{acc}(F)$ on a panel, for frontier $F$.
When each cluster contributes one row we use Newcombe's hybrid score interval for paired proportions with continuity-corrected $\phi$~\citep{newcombe1998}; otherwise a percentile cluster bootstrap over the panel's independent units ($B=10{,}000$).
In 300-replicate simulations matching our panel sizes (40--204 clusters, cluster sizes 1 and 6) these procedures covered at 0.94--0.96 for a nominal 0.95.
The primary family for H2 has four comparisons (FOLIO and ANLI, two frontiers), Bonferroni-adjusted to $\alpha=0.0125$; the secondary family (JudgeBench gpt, JudgeBench claude, Banking77-16, two frontiers) is adjusted separately to $\alpha=0.05/6$.
The H1 family has six difference-in-differences (two frontiers, depth 5 vs.\ 1, depth 3 vs.\ 1, eight vs.\ zero distractors), adjusted to $\alpha=0.05/6$ and bootstrapped over the 48 graph seeds.

The deployment conclusion for a panel and frontier follows a frozen rule with a noninferiority margin of two accuracy points:
\emph{JEV-9B more accurate} if the adjusted lower bound of $\Delta$ exceeds 0;
\emph{comparable and cheaper} if it exceeds $-0.02$ and JEV-9B's measured cost is lower;
\emph{frontier materially more accurate} if the upper bound is below $-0.02$;
otherwise \emph{inconclusive}.
A non-significant difference is never read as equivalence, and a comparison with fewer than 20 independent clusters is inconclusive.

\subsection{Routing evaluation}
\label{sec:routing}
For H3, each policy sends an item either to JEV-9B System~1 or to one frontier.
The \emph{feature rule} fits two $L_2$-regularised logistic regressions---one predicting JEV-9B correctness, one predicting frontier correctness---on seven pre-call features (log length, number of choices, number of sentences, counts of conditional words, negations, quantifiers and digits), and routes to JEV-9B when the predicted accuracy loss is under two points.
\emph{Feature + confidence} adds JEV-9B's top probability, which is available for a fraction of a cent.
Baselines: always JEV-9B; always the frontier; length only; task-family lookup (JEV-9B for a family whose training accuracy is within two points of the frontier's; unseen families go to the frontier); a confidence gate whose threshold is the lowest that keeps training accuracy within two points of always-frontier; and the oracle, an unattainable bound.
Rules are fit on all but one family and evaluated on the held-out family (leave-one-family-out over FOLIO, ANLI, JudgeBench, Banking77-16 and the controlled panel), and separately fit on everything except the unseen controlled template and evaluated on it.
Each policy is scored by accuracy and total cost, and the comparison of interest is whether a rule reaches the always-frontier accuracy band at lower cost.
